CHAPTER IV – RESULTS AND DISCUSSION

Objectives

The Kalubihan Emergency Response System was developed to provide a centralized, role-based platform for barangay emergency reporting and response coordination. Specifically, the project aimed to enable residents to file emergency reports with photo evidence and GPS location, equip dispatchers with real-time triage and assignment tools, allow departments and responders to act on dispatched incidents, and give administrators full control over user verification, roles, and page access. This chapter presents the results of the testing activities, the implementation details, the postimplementation activities, and the assessment of the system against these objectives.

Testing

The testing phase was conducted to ensure that the developed system functioned correctly, reliably, and in accordance with the specified requirements. This phase involved a series of structured testing activities aimed at identifying errors, validating system behavior, and confirming that each feature performed as intended. Different levels of testing were implemented throughout the development process to evaluate both individual system components and the system as a whole before deployment.

Unit Testing

Unit testing was performed to verify the functionality of individual system modules independently. Each module, such as user authentication, resident registration and ID verification, incident reporting, dispatch and assignment, announcement posting, and notification handling, was tested using predefined inputs to ensure that the expected outputs were produced. This testing phase helped identify and correct errors at an early stage of development, ensuring that each component functioned correctly before being integrated with other modules.

Integration Testing

Integration testing was conducted after successful unit testing to ensure that the interaction between combined system modules functioned properly. This phase focused on verifying data flow and communication between interconnected components, such as the linkage between resident incident reports and dispatcher assignments, the propagation of realtime notifications, and the upload of photo evidence to incident records. The objective of integration testing was to detect interface issues and ensure that integrated modules worked together seamlessly without causing system errors.

System Testing

System testing was carried out to evaluate the complete, integrated system's overall functionality in a controlled development environment. During this phase, the system was tested end-to-end by the developers and selected internal users, following realistic workflows from incident reporting through dispatch to resolution, to identify potential defects related to functionality, usability, and performance. Any issues discovered during system testing were documented and resolved to further improve system stability before presenting the system to actual end users for acceptance testing.

Acceptance Testing

Acceptance testing was conducted to determine whether the system met the needs and expectations of its intended users. Representatives of each user role, including the administrator, dispatcher, department admin, responder, and resident, performed realistic tasks such as filing emergency reports, triaging incidents, assigning responders, and resolving cases. Feedback gathered during this phase confirmed that the system's features aligned with the operational requirements of the barangay emergency response process and that the system was ready for deployment.

Postimplementation Activities

After the system passed acceptance testing, postimplementation activities were carried out to ensure the system's continued reliability, maintainability, and alignment with the barangay's operational needs.

System Support

System support covers the assistance provided to users after deployment. This includes onboarding new administrators and responders, resolving account and access issues through the Access control module, maintaining the seeded emergency contacts directory, and addressing user-reported problems through the project's issue-tracking process. A dedicated support contact within the development team handles escalated technical concerns.

System Maintenance

System maintenance is performed to keep the platform secure, stable, and up to date. This includes monitoring the Supabase backend for errors and quota usage, applying dependency and security patches to the frontend stack, backing up the PostgreSQL database, reviewing audit logs, and validating that realtime channels, authentication, and edge functions continue to operate correctly. Corrective maintenance addresses defects reported by users, while preventive maintenance reduces the risk of downtime during emergencies.

CHAPTER V – SUMMARY OF FINDINGS, CONCLUSIONS AND RECOMMENDATIONS

This chapter presents the conclusions drawn from the findings of the study and the recommendations proposed for future improvement. It revisits the objectives of the system, highlights the key results obtained from the system evaluation, and synthesizes their implications on the effectiveness of the developed solution. The insights derived from this chapter serve as a basis for enhancing the system and guiding future researchers in further refining and expanding the project.

Summary

Kalubihan Emergency Response System is a full-stack web-based incident management platform designed to streamline emergency reporting and response operations within the barangay. The system connects residents, dispatchers, responders, and administrators through role-based platforms that automate incident triage, responder assignment, and status tracking. Residents use a mobile-friendly interface to file emergency reports with photo evidence and GPS location, track their reports, and view announcements, while dispatchers use a web-based command center to triage incidents, assign departments, and monitor the response pipeline. Department admins and responders act on dispatched incidents, marking arrival and resolving cases with proof documentation. An admin panel oversees resident ID verification, user management, page-access control, departments, and report generation to ensure system integrity and accountability. Built using modern technologies such as React, Vite, Tailwind CSS, and Supabase with PostgreSQL, and developed following the Agile methodology, the system reduces manual coordination, accelerates dispatch, improves communication, and provides data-driven insights resulting in a more efficient, transparent, and reliable barangay emergency response ecosystem.

Conclusions

The development of the Kalubihan Emergency Response System addresses the common challenges encountered in traditional barangay emergency handling, such as delayed reporting, manual dispatch processes, inefficient communication between residents and responders, limited incident transparency, and lack of centralized records. By providing a web-based platform for administrators, dispatchers, and department responders, along with a mobile-friendly interface for residents, the system streamlines incident reporting, assignment, and resolution tracking in a more organized and efficient manner.

Kalubihan Emergency Response System integrates key features such as role-based user access, resident ID verification, incident reporting with photo and location evidence, department and responder management, realtime notifications, announcements, and reporting tools. Through the application of modern web technologies, the system improves operational efficiency, reduces human error, and enhances overall service reliability.

Based on the system evaluation results, the system scored highly across key quality metrics, especially in functional suitability, usability, and security. Users strongly agreed that the system effectively serves residents, responders, and administrators through accurate incident handling, intuitive navigation, and secure data processing. Although some minor performance and reliability issues were noted during peak usage, the overall data confirms that the platform successfully meets its core objectives.

Even with certain testing constraints and simulated deployment conditions, the research validates the feasibility and practical application of a centralized barangay emergency response system.

Recommendations

To maximize the Kalubihan Emergency Response System's potential, several key improvements are recommended. Primarily, a full-scale deployment in a real-world environment with live residents and responders is crucial for genuine performance evaluation. Feature enhancements should include integrating SMS alerts for users without stable internet access and optimizing backend performance to handle high-traffic scenarios during large-scale emergencies.

For a better user experience, refining the mobile interface into an installable application, adding incident location autocomplete, and advancing the analytics module with heatmaps and trend forecasting are advised. To support responders, integrating third-party tools for logistics like routing and turn-by-turn navigation would be beneficial.

Foundational priorities must include maintaining robust data security through encryption and audits, as well as designing the system's architecture for scalability to accommodate future growth. Lastly, establishing a cycle of regular maintenance, user feedback, and performance reviews will ensure continuous improvement. Future research could build on this work by exploring AI-driven incident triage and broader platform integrations.
