# Explainer Video — Script & Storyboard (~2.5 min)

Record your screen (OBS / Xbox Game Bar Win+G / phone over shoulder) while doing
the actions below, then read the voiceover. Two chapters: **why it exists** then
**how it works**.

---

## Chapter 1 — Why the system is needed (0:00–0:45)

**Show:** a slide or paper props — walk-in logbook, phone, radio.
Or quick shots: paper forms, a phone call being made.

**Voiceover:**
> "In Barangay Kalubihan, an emergency today is reported the slow way — a walk-in
> to the barangay hall, a phone call, a radio message. Details get lost, response
> is delayed, and there is no record of who reported what or how fast it was
> handled. Two residents can report the same fire minutes apart and nobody knows
> it's the same incident."

**Show:** title card — *Kalubihan Emergency Response System*.

**Voiceover:**
> "The Kalubihan Emergency Response System replaces all of that with one
> web platform connecting residents, dispatchers, responders, and
> administrators — in real time."

## Chapter 2 — How it actually works (0:45–2:30)

**Scene A — Resident files a report (0:45–1:15)**

**Show:** resident logs in → New Report → pick type, attach photo, GPS captures
location → the geofence check shows "inside the boundary" → Submit.

**Voiceover:**
> "A resident files a report in under a minute: the incident type, a photo as
> evidence, and an automatic GPS pin. The system verifies the location is
> inside the barangay boundary — reports outside it can't be filed. If a
> neighbor already reported the same incident nearby, the resident is warned
> that help may already be on the way."

**Scene B — Dispatcher triages (1:15–1:45)**

**Show:** dispatcher dashboard → incident queue → clustered row with ×2 badge →
open modal showing both reporters and their photos → assign responder.

**Voiceover:**
> "On the dispatcher's screen, corroborating reports of the same incident are
> automatically clustered into one queue item — one dispatch, every reporter
> kept informed. The dispatcher reviews the details, photos, and live map,
> then assigns the right department and responder with one click."

**Scene C — Responder acts (1:45–2:10)**

**Show:** responder board → accept → navigate → arrive on scene → resolve with
proof photo.

**Voiceover:**
> "The responder sees the assignment instantly, confirms arrival on scene,
> and closes the case with resolution proof — all logged on a timeline."

**Scene D — Everyone stays informed (2:10–2:30)**

**Show:** resident's My Reports flipping to "resolved" + notification bell; quick
cut to admin panel (verification queue, metrics).

**Voiceover:**
> "Every status change notifies the resident in real time — no more wondering
> if help is coming. Meanwhile, admins verify resident IDs, control access by
> role, and track operational metrics. From report to resolution, the whole
> incident lifecycle is digital, traceable, and faster than the manual process
> it replaces."

---

## Recording tips

- Do the flow once for practice, once for keeps — fresh data each run looks
  better (submit a real test report while recording).
- Two browsers side-by-side (resident + dispatcher) makes the realtime update
  visible on camera.
- Keep it under 3 minutes; the outline lists the video first, so it sets the
  panel's first impression.
- Export as `defense_video.mp4` and drop it beside `defense_presentation.html` —
  slide 2 plays it inline.
